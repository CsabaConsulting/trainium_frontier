Another idea is Byte Latent Transformers (BLT) / Multiscale Byte-Level Hierarchies ("Instead of dropping standard attention, architectures like Meta’s Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) or Multiscale Byte Language Models (MBLMs) replace the standard subword tokenizer (like BPE) with a dynamic, hierarchical byte patcher. Why it trains faster to a target bpb: Subword tokenization leaks structure and creates artificial cross-entropy boundaries. BLT groups raw bytes into dynamically-sized patches based on data entropy. Complex character structures get short patches (more compute), and highly predictable structures get massive, long patches. The Trainium Advantage: Tokenizers are notoriously painful to manage efficiently outside the host CPU. Pushing raw raw-byte matrices straight to Trainium's Tensor and Vector engines avoids string-manipulation bottlenecks. The model scales explicitly on raw data complexity, minimizing wasted FLOPs on predictable character sequences") and another technique is MatMul-Free or Low-Bit Quantized Training (e.g., Ternary LLMs / BitNet): "Architectures that eliminate or radically restrict heavy Matrix Multiplications (MatMul) scale down the precision of the weights during the training forward pass itself (e.g., to 1-bit or ternary -1, 0, 1 values). Why it trains faster to a target bpb: While low-bit models require carefully tuned scaling factors to keep bpb steady, they replace energy-expensive and memory-heavy floating-point multiplications with basic element-wise additions and bitwise shifts. The Trainium Advantage: Trainium natively implements user-programmable rounding modes, including Stochastic Rounding at the hardware level (Round Nearest Even Stochastic Rounding). Stochastic rounding is the "secret sauce" required to make low-bit or quantized training stable without collapsing the model's loss gradient. It allows tiny fractional gradient updates to register mathematically, letting a highly optimized low-bit GPT variant train at maximum hardware throughput.
